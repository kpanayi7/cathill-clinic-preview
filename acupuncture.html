Acupuncture

A calm, unhurried option for persistent pain and general wellbeing — on its own, or alongside your other treatment at the clinic.

What acupuncture helps with

Acupuncture uses very fine needles placed at specific points to stimulate the body's own responses. Patients most often come to us with pain that has outstayed its welcome — the kind that's been "managed" for years — or simply feeling run down and wanting to do something about it.

  • Persistent and recurring pain
  • Tension-type headaches
  • Muscle tightness that keeps returning
  • Stress and general wellbeing
  • Support alongside chiropractic or massage treatment

What it's actually like

Far gentler than most people expect. The needles are hair-thin — nothing like an injection — and most patients feel a brief dull sensation, then very little. Sessions are quiet and unhurried; a surprising number of people doze off. Many describe the after-effect as feeling "unknotted".

Part of a joined-up plan

At Cat Hill, acupuncture often works alongside the other therapies — settling persistent pain while chiropractic or physiotherapy addresses the mechanical cause. At your first assessment we'll be straightforward about what acupuncture can and can't do for your particular problem.

Common questions

Acupuncture FAQs

Does it hurt?
Usually no more than a brief, dull ache as each needle settles — many placements aren't felt at all. If anything feels uncomfortable, say so and it's adjusted immediately.
Are the needles safe?
All needles are sterile, single-use and disposed of after your session. Hygiene here follows the same standards as everything else in the clinic.
How many sessions before I notice anything?
Some people notice a change after the first session; for longer-standing problems it's more often three or four. We'll review honestly as we go — acupuncture isn't the right tool for everyone, and we'll say so if it isn't working for you.
