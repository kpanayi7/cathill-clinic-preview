Therapeutic Massage

Targeted soft-tissue work with Tracy Ward — for the tension you can point to and the stress you can't.

What therapeutic massage helps with

This isn't spa massage with whale music. Therapeutic massage works specific muscles with specific intent: releasing tension that's built up over months, helping tissue recover after exertion, and interrupting the stress-tightness-pain cycle that desk life feeds so well.

  • Muscle tension in the neck, shoulders and back
  • Stress-related tightness and tension headaches
  • Recovery from sport and heavy training
  • Aromatherapy massage for deeper relaxation
  • Indian head massage

How Tracy works

Tracy qualified in anatomy, physiology and body massage in 1997, later adding aromatherapy (IIHHT) and Indian head massage (ITEC) to her training. She'll ask what you need before every session — deep, specific work on a problem area one visit, a full-body reset the next — and adjusts pressure to you, not to a routine.

Working alongside the other therapies

Massage often runs alongside chiropractic care at Cat Hill: soft tissue relaxes, adjustments hold better, and progress speeds up. If Tracy thinks your problem needs Jenny's or James's eyes instead, they're in the same building — that's rather the point of us.

Common questions

Massage FAQs

How firm is the pressure?
As firm as helps and no firmer — Tracy checks in as she works, and "effective" doesn't have to mean "gritting your teeth". Tell her your preference and she'll work to it.
Massage or chiropractic — which do I need?
Rough rule: if the problem feels like tight muscle, start with massage; if it feels like a joint that won't move or pain that travels, start with chiropractic. If you're not sure, message us or ask at reception — nobody minds pointing you to the other room.
How often should I come?
For a specific problem, sessions closer together at first, spacing out as it settles. For maintenance and stress management, every four to six weeks suits most people.
